What changed

  • Classic ground animation: the native animation queue resolves INCBIN markers to complete image frames before VBlank, retains the preceding frame if an asset read fails, and marks only affected VRAM tiles for existing 2D/voxel texture updates. This corrects the remaining flower/ground speckles and water/shoreline animation. Map geometry is not rebuilt, and staged field-animation updates are skipped during battles.
  • Native battle composition: the FireRed/FRLG compositor snapshots emulated display registers once per scanline/pass, uses a fast path for uniform window masks and restricts object-window work to relevant sprites/scanlines. General split, wrapped, object and affine paths remain available.
  • Native battle UI and uploads: lower-screen redraws cover changed header/body/footer regions; rounded primitives use equivalent contiguous vertical spans. Changed-texture uploads are queued after successful FrameBegin, and upload-source buffers remain valid until the next completed GPU frame. V3 compares UI source rows, converts only changed rows and coalesces adjacent dirty-row cache flushes. The full queued texture transfer, layout, filtering and resolution remain unchanged. Lower-screen framebuffer cache flushing still covers full affected columns; this is separate from the sparse texture-staging flushes.
  • FireRed V4 menu glyph cache: a bounded 128-entry cache reuses repeated glyph work, preserving small-font scratch behavior and reset/init invalidation. Its real-font differential test, CI, fresh rebuild and Thor gates passed. This FireRed-only change is not part of the qualified FRLG+ V3 runtime.

The earlier Medicine/five-pocket, forest-border and PP layout corrections remain in these sources. Their final-device regression results are recorded below; no encounters, battle rules or save formats are changed.

Thor protocol and interpretation

The cartridge release gate has three independently required scenes. Steady battle selection retains the first 12 complete 300-game-frame PROF windows and 5 complete 600-game-frame PACE windows, requiring at least 10% reduction in the reported work-window median versus the original unoptimized baseline. Every retained candidate drawn-FPS window must be at least 59.6, and before/after game cadence must be within 59.6–60.0 FPS.

The controlled interaction segment uses the same script before and after: 52 alternating A (open Fight) and B (Back) inputs at 1.5-second spacing over 78 seconds, with no attacks or screenshots during measurement. It retains 12 complete PROF and 5 complete PACE windows. The blinking-text segment holds at “Wild appeared!” for 45 seconds without inputs or screenshots and retains 6 PROF / 3 PACE windows. Both require every candidate drawn window to be at least 59.6 and both builds' game cadence within 59.6–60.0; neither has an additional work-reduction threshold. Their before build is the initial scanline-only candidate (V1), not the original unoptimized build.

The first potentially partial aggregate is excluded independently for each counter. Work values below are medians of 300-frame window averages, not medians of individual frames. PROF work is elapsed BeginFrame-to-EndFrame time minus the recorded FrameBegin wait, clamped to zero. It is neither pure CPU time nor GPU duration. On hidden frames, the recorded wait can remain from a previous rendered frame. Moving FrameBegin before upload also shifts some synchronization from reported work into excluded wait or queued GPU work: the formula is unchanged, but its wait boundary moved. A percentage change in reported work is not an equal percentage CPU saving. Independent PACE counters establish presentation cadence.

Section timings are inclusive and can overlap; a fallback upload inside draw contributes to both sections. The scanline section includes software composition, UI selection and HBlank callbacks/DMA. Counts of logged work over 16.715 ms are not counts of dropped presentations. The slow log records work rounded to 0.01 ms, starts above 15 ms and has a 4,000-line launch cap; the completed samples listed here did not reach that cap.

Classic has different FPS semantics: its logged value is the latest roughly one-second renderer-rate estimate, sampled every 600 presented frames. It is not a 600-frame average or a separate game-FPS counter. Classic retains 8 field and 12 battle PROF windows. Its gate permits at most a 1.0 ms increase in each reported work-window median, requires unchanged cadence and at least 59.6 FPS in every candidate renderer sample, and requires clean ground animation in both 2D and voxel views.

The interaction script SHA-256 is c48ee6fdf92d172f941ee05916a14d4cd8ae2475a2f4d92ff93e38f8fdecfd8c. Actual event times must stay within 0.25 seconds of the planned schedule. Metric endpoint labels and raw log hashes are bound to current captures; audio is recomputed from raw dumps. Active-move review is bound to the exact version, cartridge and active log hashes. Actual moves/status/return, audio and visual checks are additional requirements, not inferred from a steady-scene pass.

Host tests and scope

Classic's strict C99 regression compiles the actual patched animation queue and transfer functions. It covers distinct/successive frames, dirty notifications, missing assets, queue bounds, the largest simultaneous primary/secondary update and no file I/O during VBlank. Its fresh rebuild regenerated all four scenery outputs and checked 19 complete flower/water-edge frame assets for expected size and nontrivial pixels.

The native compositor differential harness compares the full pre-optimization and optimized renderers against identical synthetic GBA memory. All 600 cases passed for full-only, UI-only and combined drawing; split/wrapped/object windows, blending, sprite mosaic and simulated HBlank/VCount register changes. Hidden/no-draw mode and background mosaic are outside that harness. Its DMA stub verifies 160 calls per frame; real DMA behavior was source-audited, not differentially emulated. A separate 602-case local audit adds live affine-OAM changes during simulated HBlank and compares pixels and final OAM/registers. Frame-cached object membership was already baseline behavior.

Lower-screen tests passed 1,200 old/new primitive cases and 2,003 full-versus-dirty battle-state transitions. Shapes/layouts/dirty decisions are production code; fonts and icons are deterministic stand-ins. Page/menu invalidation was source-reviewed rather than executed by that harness. Upload tests cover exact colour, alpha, 2× expansion, padding, dirty edges, clear/restore and 100 alternating-buffer updates, including asynchronous source-buffer lifetime, held UI, failed FrameBegin and voxel fallback. Full texture output is compared after sparse updates. These host tests are not a PICA emulator or physical-console certification; Thor results remain required.

FireRed V4's additional real-font differential passed 9,572 full-canvas, width, scratch-state and palette cases using repository Latin fonts and production decompression. Deliberately broken cache variants failed those checks. Fixtures come from bootstrapped public font PNGs and upstream conversion/decompression code, with no private ROM-derived fixtures. These checks are separate from the earlier lower-UI tests that use font stand-ins. A 12-character centered-label example required 24 uncached glyph decompressions, 8 on a cold cache and none across 100 warm repeats; these are operation counts, not FPS measurements.

These are targeted early-game scene tests. They do not repeat the full climate survey or certify every map, move effect, scripted battle or full playthrough. No global locked-60 or zero-stutter claim is made.
